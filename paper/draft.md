# AdaColRAG: Fast Query-Aware Visual Token Selection with Confidence-Aware Recovery for Efficient Document Retrieval

> **Anonymous WSDM 2027 manuscript source.** This Markdown draft maps to the ACM `sigconf` anonymous review format. Finance EN is used as the development collection for sensitivity analysis; Industrial, Pharmaceuticals, and Finance FR are held-out evaluation collections. Tables between `AUTO` markers are generated by `scripts/run_all_required_experiments.sh` and must not be edited manually.

## Abstract

Visual document retrievers preserve page layout, tables, charts, and figures by encoding rendered pages directly. Multi-vector models such as ColPali retain fine-grained evidence through token-level late interaction, but exhaustive scoring scales with both corpus size and the number of visual tokens per page. We present **AdaColRAG**, a training-free two-stage retriever that combines dense candidate generation with fast query-aware visual-token selection. A VisRAG representation first retrieves a small page set. For each candidate, a fixed query-time budget is applied after relevance prefiltering, followed by a maximal-marginal-relevance-style selector that suppresses redundant tokens and encourages spatial coverage. ColPali late interaction reranks the selected evidence, dense and multi-vector scores are fused, and low-confidence queries selectively trigger full-token recovery. A verified Finance EN study showed that diversity-aware selection, dense fusion, and recovery were the effective components, whereas a query-complexity budget controller did not significantly outperform a matched fixed budget. We therefore remove adaptive budgeting from the primary method and evaluate the resulting fixed-budget fast selector through identical-candidate controls, paired query-level bootstrap intervals, and repeated timing. The final protocol uses Finance EN only for sensitivity analysis and freezes the selected configuration before evaluation on Industrial, Pharmaceuticals, and French financial documents. This design separates candidate-generation, token-selection, fusion, and recovery effects while retaining complete checkpoint provenance and query-level traces.

**Keywords:** visual document retrieval; multimodal retrieval; late interaction; visual-token selection; maximal marginal relevance; selective computation; retrieval-augmented generation

## 1 Introduction

Enterprise search increasingly operates over reports, presentations, invoices, forms, and scientific documents whose relevant evidence is not fully represented by plain text. Table structure, chart geometry, typography, and spatial associations can determine whether a page answers a query. OCR-first pipelines can discard these signals or propagate parsing errors. Visual document retrieval instead treats the rendered page as the primary retrieval unit [1--10].

ColPali [1] adapts ColBERT-style late interaction [11, 12] to page images. A page is represented by many visual embeddings, and every query token retrieves its strongest matching page token. This mechanism preserves local evidence but makes full-corpus scoring expensive. A page in our exported ColPali index contains 1,024 visual tokens; scoring thousands of pages therefore requires millions of page-token operations for every query.

Storage-oriented compression cannot observe future queries. Light-ColPali [5], for example, studies index-time patch reduction and shows that irreversible pruning can remove evidence needed later. We consider the complementary online setting. The complete multi-vector index remains unchanged, but only a query-conditioned subset of tokens is scored for a small candidate set. The system may recover full tokens when the compressed ranking appears unreliable.

An initial verified Finance EN study produced three findings that determine the final method. First, a query-complexity budget controller did not significantly outperform a fixed budget with comparable mean token use. Second, relevance--diversity selection significantly improved retrieval at the same token count. Third, dense-score fusion and confidence-aware recovery contributed more consistently than budget adaptation. We therefore remove adaptive budgeting from the primary claim and adopt a fixed 112-token operating point selected on Finance EN. The legacy controller remains in the repository only for reproducibility of archived runs.

AdaColRAG now combines four mechanisms. First, VisRAG-Ret [2] supplies dense Top-50 candidates. Second, query relevance forms a moderate prefilter pool, avoiding expensive MMR over all 1,024 page tokens. Third, a fast MMR selector [18] balances relevance, redundancy suppression, and approximate page coverage under a fixed budget. Fourth, a selective-prediction policy [19] uses ranking and evidence confidence to invoke Top-100 full-token recovery only when needed. Dense and late-interaction scores are fused to exploit complementary global and local evidence.

Our contributions are:

1. **Fast query-aware visual-token selection.** We introduce relevance-prefiltered MMR for visual late interaction, reducing selector cost while retaining diversity and layout coverage.
2. **A controlled dense--multi-vector retrieval pipeline.** Identical-candidate experiments separate candidate generation, token selection, score fusion, and recovery.
3. **Confidence-aware full-token recovery.** The system selectively restores complete page evidence and counts both compressed and recovery passes in all efficiency measurements.
4. **Evidence-driven simplification.** A matched-budget experiment shows that adaptive budgeting is not yet a supported contribution; the final method uses a fixed development-selected budget and reports the negative result.
5. **A reproducible cross-domain protocol.** Verified merged checkpoints, pinned datasets, query-level traces, paired bootstrap intervals, repeated timing, and automatic manuscript tables are included in one executable workflow.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Visual document retrieval

Document Screenshot Embedding [4] represents rendered pages with a single visual vector, whereas ColPali [1] uses multi-vector late interaction to retain fine-grained evidence. VisRAG [2] integrates direct visual retrieval into multimodal RAG. UniSE [6] studies unified screenshot representations, Unveil [7] distills visual and textual evidence into a parsing-free retriever, and recent systems address iterative visual RAG, long documents, and zero-shot retrieval [8--10]. ViDoRe V3 [3] broadens evaluation to professional domains and multilingual queries.

The closest efficiency study is Light-ColPali [5], which investigates query-independent pruning and token merging for reducing index storage. AdaColRAG retains the complete index and instead reduces query-time scoring work after candidate generation. Index compression and online selection target different resources and can be combined.

### 2.2 Dense and late-interaction retrieval

Dense retrieval [13, 28--30] maps a query and document to one vector, enabling efficient large-scale candidate search. Late-interaction systems such as ColBERT and ColBERTv2 [11, 12] preserve multiple contextualized vectors and improve fine-grained matching at a higher scoring cost. AdaColRAG explicitly combines the two: dense vectors retrieve candidates and selected visual vectors perform local evidence matching.

### 2.3 Token reduction and efficient visual inference

DynamicViT [24], token reorganization [25], TokenLearner [26], and Token Merging [27] reduce computation inside vision models. They typically operate during image encoding and are not conditioned on a retrieval query. AdaColRAG leaves the frozen encoder and stored index unchanged. It selects previously exported page tokens according to the current query and measures effects on ranking, recovery, and end-to-end retrieval work.

### 2.4 Selective computation and uncertainty

Selective prediction invokes a more expensive action when a cheaper prediction is insufficient [19]. Retrieval systems can estimate uncertainty from margins, score distributions, or retriever agreement. AdaColRAG combines top-rank margin, inverse entropy, and query-evidence coverage. Unlike an oracle fallback, the decision uses only scores and selected-token evidence available at retrieval time.

## 3 Problem Formulation

Let a query be represented by normalized token embeddings

\[
Q=[q_1,\ldots,q_m]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},
\]

and a page by normalized visual embeddings

\[
D=[d_1,\ldots,d_n]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}.
\]

Full late interaction computes

\[
s_{\mathrm{LI}}(Q,D)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}q_i^\top d_j.
\]

For corpus size \(N\), exhaustive scoring has work proportional to \(O(Nmnd)\). A two-stage retriever selects \(C\ll N\) candidates and a page-token subset \(S_Q(D)\) of fixed size \(K\ll n\):

\[
\tilde{s}_{\mathrm{LI}}(Q,D)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max_{j\in S_Q(D)}q_i^\top d_j.
\]

We distinguish two efficiency quantities. **Local token reduction** measures compression within the page-scoring operations actually performed:

\[
R_{\mathrm{local}}=1-\frac{\sum_q T_q}{\sum_q T_q^{\mathrm{full}}}.
\]

**System visual-token work reduction** compares actual page-token work with exhaustive full-token ColPali:

\[
R_{\mathrm{system}}=1-\frac{\mathbb{E}_q[T_q]}{N\bar n},
\]

where \(\bar n\) is the mean stored token count per page. This metric combines candidate pruning and token selection; it is not an index-storage reduction.

The goal is not to satisfy a single compression threshold. We seek Pareto-efficient configurations that improve or preserve retrieval quality while reducing measured latency and token work under an auditable recovery policy.

## 4 Method

### 4.1 Dense candidate generation

VisRAG-Ret supplies normalized vectors \(u_Q\) and \(u_D\). Cosine similarity retrieves the Top-\(C\) pages, with \(C=50\) in the main method. A controlled alternative uses normalized mean-pooled ColPali tokens:

\[
\bar q=\operatorname{norm}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_iq_i\right),\qquad
\bar d=\operatorname{norm}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_jd_j\right).
\]

Candidate-source controls use full-token and fixed-token ColPali scoring after either mean-vector or VisRAG candidate generation.

### 4.2 Fixed query-time budget

The final method uses a fixed budget \(K=112\), selected on Finance EN before held-out evaluation. This decision follows a matched-budget result in which the previous complexity controller did not significantly outperform Fixed-112. The fixed budget improves causal interpretation: selector variants consume the same number of tokens, and differences can be attributed to which tokens are retained rather than how many are retained.

The previous adaptive controller remains available as an archived diagnostic but is excluded from the main matrix and paper contributions. Budget sensitivity at 64, 96, 112, and 128 tokens is reported only on Finance EN.

### 4.3 Relevance prefiltering

For page token \(d_j\), query relevance is

\[
r_j=\max_i q_i^\top d_j.
\]

Applying exact greedy MMR to all 1,024 tokens is costly. We first retain the Top-\(P\) tokens under \(r_j\), where

\[
P=\min(n,\gamma K),
\]

and \(\gamma=4\) in the primary configuration. Development experiments compare \(\gamma\in\{2,4\}\) with exact MMR over all tokens. The relevance-only baseline returns the Top-\(K\) tokens directly.

### 4.4 Fast redundancy and layout selection

Within the prefilter pool, selection begins with the highest-relevance token and greedily chooses

\[
j^*=\arg\max_{j\notin S}
\left[
\lambda_r r_j
-\lambda_c\max_{k\in S}d_j^\top d_k
+\lambda_l g(j,S)
\right].
\]

The redundancy term suppresses near-duplicate visual evidence. The layout term \(g(j,S)\) rewards approximate spatial bins that are underrepresented. Maximum redundancy is updated incrementally, so each iteration compares candidates only with the newest selected token. Main ablations separately enable redundancy, layout, and their combination under identical candidates and token budgets.

The exported token coordinates are approximate normalized grid positions reconstructed from token order. They support a coverage heuristic but are not interpreted as exact semantic regions.

### 4.5 Dense and late-interaction fusion

Selected-token late-interaction scores and dense scores are min--max normalized within the candidate set:

\[
s_{\mathrm{fuse}}=(1-\alpha)\hat{s}_{\mathrm{LI}}+\alpha\hat{s}_{\mathrm{dense}},
\]

with \(\alpha=0.15\), selected on Finance EN. The main matrix includes the same fast-MMR configuration with and without fusion. An OCR lexical extension is reported separately and is not part of the primary method.

### 4.6 Confidence-aware recovery

Confidence combines top-rank margin, inverse normalized entropy, and query-evidence coverage. For the current top page,

\[
c_{\mathrm{cov}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_i
\mathbf{1}\left[
\max_{j\in S_Q(D_1)}q_i^\top d_j\geq\tau_s
\right].
\]

The final confidence is

\[
c_Q=w_mc_{\mathrm{margin}}+w_ec_{\mathrm{entropy}}+w_vc_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\]

When \(c_Q<\tau_c\), AdaColRAG scores the Top-100 dense candidates with full page tokens. Both the initial compressed pass and the recovery pass are included in latency, scoring-operation, and token-work totals. Finance EN development experiments compare \(\tau_c\in\{0.44,0.48,0.52\}\); the frozen main value is 0.48.

## 5 Experimental Protocol

### 5.1 Development and held-out collections

We use four ViDoRe V3 collections [3]. Finance EN is the only development collection. It is used to select the fixed token budget, MMR prefilter factor, fusion weight, and recovery threshold. Industrial, Pharmaceuticals, and Finance FR are held-out evaluations; their labels do not influence the frozen configuration.

| Dataset | Domain | Language | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| ViDoRe V3 Finance EN | annual financial reports | English | development and continuity with the verified pilot |
| ViDoRe V3 Industrial | industrial technical documents | English | held-out domain transfer |
| ViDoRe V3 Pharmaceuticals | pharmaceutical documents | English | held-out scientific/table transfer |
| ViDoRe V3 Finance FR | financial reports | French | held-out multilingual transfer |

Dataset revisions and raw parquet shard lists are pinned in the one-click workflow. Generated manifests report page, query, qrel, and checksum counts.

### 5.2 Encoders and provenance

The fine retriever is the official merged checkpoint `vidore/colpali-v1.3-merged`. It is downloaded to a verified local snapshot and loaded with `local_files_only=True`. The dense retriever is `openbmb/VisRAG-Ret`, also preferably loaded from a local snapshot. ColPali pages and queries are exported as normalized multi-vector arrays; VisRAG pages and queries are exported as normalized single vectors. All experiments for a dataset reuse identical exported embeddings.

The workflow records Git commit, CPU, GPU, CUDA, Python and package versions, BLAS thread settings, dataset checksums, model manifests, query-level rankings, token counts, confidence values, and fallback decisions.

### 5.3 Main controlled matrix

The main matrix contains 13 systems.

| ID | Candidate generation | Fine scoring | Fusion | Recovery | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `visrag_dense` | VisRAG, full corpus | none | dense only | no | external single-vector baseline |

| `visrag_top50_fixed_112` | VisRAG Top-50 | relevance Top-112 | no | no | matched token baseline |
| `visrag_top50_mmr_redundancy` | VisRAG Top-50 | fixed-112, redundancy only | no | no | redundancy ablation |
| `visrag_top50_mmr_layout` | VisRAG Top-50 | fixed-112, layout only | no | no | layout ablation |

All selector comparisons use the same candidate set and 112-token budget. This removes candidate and token-count confounding.

### 5.4 Finance EN development matrix

Finance EN runs a separate diagnostic matrix:

- fixed budgets: 64, 96, 112, and 128;
- MMR prefilter factors: 2, 4, and exact all-token MMR;
- confidence thresholds: 0.44, 0.48, and 0.52;
- dense fusion weights: 0.05, 0.15, and 0.25.

These results determine whether the selected configuration lies on a reasonable quality--latency frontier. They are not pooled with held-out evidence.

### 5.5 Metrics and statistical analysis

Retrieval metrics are nDCG@5, nDCG@10, Recall@1/5/10, and MRR@10. Efficiency metrics are local token reduction, system visual-token work reduction, page-scoring operations per query, mean/P50/P95 retrieval-stage latency, and repeated wall-clock time. Reliability metrics are fallback rate, mean confidence, and fallback/non-fallback cost decomposition.

For every planned comparison, we compute a paired query-level bootstrap interval for nDCG@5 with 10,000 resamples. We report the mean difference, 95% interval, two-sided bootstrap probability, and fraction of queries improved. Quality--latency Pareto membership is computed within each dataset.

Latency covers coarse scoring, token selection, late interaction, fusion, recovery, and sorting over preloaded embeddings. It excludes image/query encoding, disk loading, network transfer, and downstream generation. One warm-up and three measured repetitions are used for selected systems in the full protocol.

## 6 Results

### 6.1 Verified Finance EN evidence motivating the redesign

The verified merged-checkpoint Finance EN study established that the overall direction is viable. AdaColRAG reached approximately 0.4825 nDCG@5 versus 0.4387 for exhaustive ColPali. The paired difference was positive with a bootstrap interval above zero. Under identical VisRAG Top-50 candidates, diversity-aware selection improved over relevance-only selection at the same mean token use, while adaptive budgeting did not significantly improve over Fixed-112. Dense fusion produced a larger and statistically supported gain, and recovery showed a positive but less certain effect. These observations motivate the simplified fixed-budget method; they are not substitutes for the new fast-MMR cross-domain run.

## 7 Reproducibility and Implementation

The repository separates model-specific export environments from a NumPy retrieval environment. The one-click workflow:

1. verifies or downloads local ColPali and VisRAG snapshots;
2. downloads pinned dataset shards and creates normalized manifests;
3. exports or reuses ColPali and VisRAG embeddings;
4. runs Finance EN development diagnostics;
5. runs the 13-system main matrix on all four datasets;
6. validates checkpoint and result integrity;
7. computes paired bootstrap intervals and Pareto flags;
8. repeats selected timing measurements;
9. aggregates cross-dataset tables and injects them into this manuscript.

Fast MMR uses deterministic relevance prefiltering and incremental maximum-redundancy updates. `prefilter_factor=0` reproduces exact archived MMR. The legacy adaptive-budget controller remains available for reproducibility but is not used by the primary method or main matrix.

## 8 Limitations and Threats to Validity

First, Finance EN is used for development and therefore cannot provide unbiased evidence for the chosen operating point. The held-out conclusions must rely on Industrial, Pharmaceuticals, and Finance FR. Second, approximate token coordinates support only coarse coverage and should not be interpreted as exact layout semantics. Third, the complete ColPali index remains stored; AdaColRAG reduces scoring work, not index size. Fourth, dense-only VisRAG may remain faster or better on some recall metrics, so the method should be presented as a quality--cost operating point rather than universal domination. Fifth, NumPy retrieval timing excludes encoder inference and production-serving overhead. Sixth, recovery duplicates the initial compressed work and can dominate cost if the confidence threshold is poorly calibrated. Seventh, Finance EN sensitivity examines a limited grid rather than a continuous or learned policy. Finally, cross-domain claims are valid only if the frozen configuration is not retuned on held-out labels.

## 9 Ethical Considerations

The benchmark uses publicly released professional documents and retrieval annotations. The method does not generate financial, industrial, or pharmaceutical advice. Retrieval errors may omit qualifying evidence or surface misleading pages; downstream systems should preserve page provenance and allow inspection of original documents. Private deployments must protect queries, traces, and retrieved pages through access controls and retention policies. Dataset and model licenses must be respected, and efficiency claims should state the hardware, software, and measurement boundary under which they were obtained.

## 10 Conclusion

AdaColRAG treats visual-token scoring as a query-time retrieval policy. The final method uses dense candidate generation, a development-selected fixed budget, relevance-prefiltered MMR, dense/late-interaction fusion, and confidence-aware full-token recovery. This design is deliberately narrower than the original adaptive-budget proposal: the unsupported component is reported as a negative result and removed from the main claim. The resulting experiment structure isolates each effective module under identical candidates and token budgets, while Finance EN development is separated from three held-out domain and language evaluations. Final claims will be determined by the automatically generated cross-domain, bootstrap, and repeated-timing results.
